# Review answers and decide when to finish

## After each answer

- Distinguish observed facts and their sources, the user's decisions and reasons, and explanations that still need evidence. For conditional answers such as “only during our busy season,” record when they apply. If different interpretations would change the direction, ask for a concrete example next.
- Update other questions the user answered along the way. If an answer conflicts with an earlier one, check whether both could be true for different people, situations, or periods. Ask one question about any remaining discrepancy. When a premise changes, revisit the explanation of the problem, the ranking of options, and any success criteria that depended on it.
- For a deferred question, record the missing fact, the decision it affects, and the evidence or answer needed to resume. Work on independent questions and reopen the deferred one when new evidence arrives. If the user pauses, stop asking and leave a summary of agreements and the point to resume in the conversation.

## When there are no more questions queued

Compare the latest summary with the original request and sources. For a representative case, can you explain who encounters the problem, why the proposed direction would help, and how someone would observe an improvement? Would the same decision hold in a case with different conditions? If it changes, can you explain where and why the direction applies? Investigate unsupported steps, contradictions, or counterexamples that could change the conclusion, or ask one question about the premise. Repeat the review after the answer.

For each remaining unknown, ask whether a different answer would change the direction, scope, or how improvement is judged. If so, investigate what you can and ask about facts only the user can establish. If the available material leaves a material fact unresolved, mark it “Unconfirmed (user confirmation required).” State which decision depends on it and what evidence would allow the investigation to resume. Carry forward details that can be decided later and explain why they can wait.

## Confirm the outcome

Summarize the evidence and open questions. If the user has yet to decide, present viable outcomes in order of recommendation, ask for one decision, and wait for the reply.

- **Proceed.** Once the review has resolved the unknowns that could change the direction and the user has chosen the scope and approach, summarize the situation and its basis, how to check for improvement, scope, rationale, and details to settle later.
- **Investigate further.** Identify the question, what evidence would answer it, and the decision to revisit. Start another round if it can be investigated now. If outside material or a reply is needed, record what to bring back and pause.
- **Set the idea aside.** Record the user's reason and any conditions they gave for reconsidering.

If the user corrects a premise or offers an alternative during final confirmation, revisit the decisions it affects. Once material unknowns are resolved, an explicit decision to proceed is sufficient for the handoff. If the user pauses or sets the idea aside, summarize the agreements and remaining questions at that point.
